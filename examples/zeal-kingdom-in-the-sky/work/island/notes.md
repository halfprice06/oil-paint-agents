# Island (work/island) — notes (round 4)

**Artist statement.** The floating island is stratified rock hanging in gold light: three strata that step in and out, their overhanging ledges catching warm ochre-pink light at the sun-struck left and fading to violet-grey and a near-black blue-brown core at the lower right, every bottom edge lifted by pink-lavender bounce from the sunlit cloud sea; one broad white fall that bursts on a ledge and splits into two sheets, two narrower falls, and thin drifting spray veils; on top a garden seen at a shallow angle, lawns in bands of green, a pale stone terrace and stair at the palace front, paths and low stone walls with flower beds, a stream winding to the main fall, trees with thick lit boughs and long cool shadows, and slender cypresses for scale.

## Self-assessment
What works: the underside now reads as stratified rock (three strata with overhanging ledges, dark undersides and lit outer edges, a stepped lower-right silhouette, a long central stalactite), warm sienna-ochre at the lit left to a near-black blue-brown core at the lower right, with bounce light along the bottom edges. The main fall is the drama: a wide crest, a broad sheet, a white burst on the second stratum and two sheets below that dissolve into veils. The plateau is a garden: banded lawns, a stone terrace and stair at the palace front, paths, low walls with flower beds, the stream, trees with thick lit boughs and long shadows, cypresses for scale, and rim trees breaking the back edge.
What still gives it away (round 4): at 2x the fall ribbons are still partly dry-brush strands with dark gaps rather than one sheet of water; the lit left face can read as stacked planks where the flat strokes line up; the centre band is a pile of rounded patches; the burst is a cluster of touches. Earlier: the first cloud wreath swallowed the whole lower half of the island and had to be cut to a third; the one that shipped is a narrow band and still reads as horizontal streaks in places; the ledge burst on the main fall is a pale cluster of touches rather than thrown spray; stroke count stays around 5,700 instead of the 8,000-11,000 asked for; every density pass I tried (minor strata rows, a dense second rock layer, a striated texture layer) turned the rock into masonry, bricks or fur, so I removed them and kept the strokes that build planes. The spray veils are horizontal smears rather than drifting mist; the stalactite tips are dark blobs; the stair and terrace are summary (a grey band with pale risers); the trees' lit boughs are still three nested arcs and can read as croissants up close; the lawn, though banded, is still a fairly even green at thumbnail size.
