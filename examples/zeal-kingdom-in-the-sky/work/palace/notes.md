# Palace area (passes 40-49)

**Artist statement.** Zeal Palace at golden hour: a pale stone drum and a smaller drum above it, each turned as a cylinder (thick warm cream in the light, thin lavender-blue in the shadow, warm reflected light low down), under an ogee spire tiled blue-green that goes to deep ultramarine on its shadow side, with a gold band and a slender gold finial catching the last sun. Cast shadows run as violet glazes down to the lower right across the terrace and the plateau. The sharpest, brightest edges are kept at the spire tip and the lit rim of the big drum; the right side is let go into the sky.

## Round 3 (after critique: taller, Zeal's architecture, no patterns)

Changes: lower drum raised (rim at y 600, wall 636-765); the dome replaced by a slender ogee spire (waist ~110 px at y 400, swelling to the rim at y 500, tip 240, gold finial to 124); tower roofs raised into ogee spires (apex 395 / 445) with gold tips; a lantern tower behind the right tower (x 1858-1898, spire apex 540) for the skyline; an arcade of pointed arches round the base of the lower drum with lit left jambs; a grand entrance with a stair spilling onto the plateau (upper treads and both balustrades); a terrace walk with a low balustrade; bridges joining each tower to the drum; windows fewer, grouped and varied, nearly lost in shadow; the dome's tile courses, masonry blocks, cornice block ring and sill dots removed (three or four broken dry-brush courses on the lit sides only); roofs shaded as cones with a melted turn and one crisp highlight ridge; the left pavilion got a small blue-green dome and an arcade, the right one a lower row of arches, lower contrast, half sunk in its shadow. The block-in's old, wider dome is covered with sky at the start of pass 42.

## Round 4 (after the first merge)

The sky patch over the old dome is repainted with the sky painter's own mixes (the same key colours and the darker-around-the-spire mix), long near-horizontal broken strokes (stir .35-.5) clipped to the old dome plus a margin, then a light soft melt. Arcade openings now have a crisp pointed top (two short strokes meeting), a thick cream left jamb, and a deep warm dark inside (burnt umber, ultramarine, a little alizarin); shadow-side arches partly dropped. Window jambs are one thick cream touch per window on the sunlit side only. The terrace balustrade has varied spacing and size, a third dropped, the coping as long lit strokes, the shadow half lost. The big drum's turn gets four melt bands and warm reflected light laid wet low on the shadow side. Both towers cast long cool glazes to the lower right (on the terrace's left part, the drum's lower wall, across the plateau).

## Round 5

The sky patch at the start of pass 42 is gone (the block-in's dome is now a thin wash and the sky painter's sky covers it in the merge). The spire has one crisp lit edge along its left contour from the finial to the rim (three thick clean turquoise-white strokes, taper 0), its right contour left soft. The gold rim band is now four broken thick knife/filbert touches on the lit side with a dark warm (burnt umber, ochre, ultramarine) accent under them, fading to one faint ochre stroke on the shadow side. Totals: 7986 strokes with the block-in, ~7060 mine.

## Self-assessment (round 3)

Works: the silhouette is now a skyline of four spires with the great ogee at the centre, taller than wide; the lit stone is thick cream against thin violet shadows with a melted terminator; the arcade, stair, balustrade and bridges make it architecture rather than a drum; the gold band and finials catch the sun; the long cast shadow sits on the plateau.

Still gives it away: at 2x the arcade's arches are blobby dark lumps rather than crisp pointed openings; the lit jambs beside windows are short pale dashes; the balustrade is a row of similar small verticals; the sky patch over the old dome is a slightly different blue from the block-in sky around it (the sky painter should paint up to the new spire silhouette); the halftone-to-shadow turn on the big drum is still a little abrupt; the wall surface is dense vertical patches that could read as a comb at 3x.
