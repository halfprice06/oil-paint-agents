# Pond Under the Poplars, Late Afternoon

**Artist statement.** Two trees stand against a soft evening sky over a still pond, the low sun behind a cream-lit cumulus at upper right, so the crowns are warm and light on their right sides and sink into cool blue-green shadow on the left. A sliver of pale meadow separates the big tree from its slimmer neighbour; the far shore dissolves into one pale grey-blue tree; the pond carries the sky and a dark pulled-down reflection of the trees; a warm-topped knoll and a dark bank frame the foot of the picture. The value plan is Corot's: a dark mass on the left balanced by an open, luminous right.

**Self-assessment.** About 1,308 strokes (round 4; earlier rounds in src/round1-3). This round: lit foliage was halved and rebuilt as 1-3 big curved strokes per mass with a few small broken touches at the edge and one blend toward the halftone, brightest only at the top right; trunks vary in width, are darker at the base with a lit sliver, and one dies into the foliage; the far tree is greyer and lighter; the meadow has broken warm strokes at varied angles and cool shadow patches under the trees; the water is lighter toward the far bank with broken strokes of different values and ripple lines; the knoll reads as one form (warm lit plane, cool face, darker foot, reflection); the cloud underside is one cool grey-violet blend with no pink.

What still gives it away: the lit foliage masses are still smooth flat-coloured blotches with little internal leaf structure and a slightly acid yellow-green; the trunks are short and stubby with dark rounded feet; the meadow's warm strokes look like straw; the water strokes are still very regular horizontals; the knoll is a stack of parallel smooth bands; the sky notches in the crown read as pale scratches; and the overall finish is cleaner and more even than a real plein-air sketch.
